The Keeping of Time

Long before mechanical clocks, ancient civilizations relied on the movement of water to measure the passage of time. Early water clocks, known as clepsydras, marked hours by the steady flow of water from one vessel to another.

Unlike sundials, water clocks functioned at night and in cloudy weather. Their chief advantage was consistency: the rate of flow remained constant regardless of light or season. This reliability made them essential in courts, where speeches were timed, and in temples, where rituals followed strict schedules.

By the medieval period, engineers had added gears and floats, transforming simple vessels into elaborate machines that rang bells and moved figurines — the direct ancestors of the mechanical clock.
